1. Start here
What is the Agentic Commerce Hackathon?
Most AI products stop after giving an answer. This hackathon is about what happens when an agent can finish the job.

Over one focused weekend, build a working product in which an AI agent can discover options, make a decision and complete or enable a transaction using Prava. The product could shop across merchants, book travel, manage a subscription, procure software, reserve a table, automate a business payment or create a new commerce experience.

We are looking for products and experiments that show what becomes possible when software can take action with money - safely, clearly and with a real purpose.

What we expect you to build
A working end-to-end experience, not only a concept, deck or collection of screens.
A clear user and problem. The product should make sense without a long explanation.
A meaningful Prava integration that enables the core commercial action.
Original thinking. The RFH library is inspiration, not a list of ideas to copy.
A product your team can explain, demonstrate and defend.
Clear disclosure of anything that existed before the hackathon.
A narrow product built extremely well can be stronger than a broad product with many unfinished features. Creative simulations and experiments are welcome when the hypothesis, execution and learning are clear.

What qualifies as a strong project?
A strong project combines five things:

A real problem: Even if only a small group needs it, those users should care about the outcome.
A complete action: The agent should do meaningful work and reach a visible result.
An original insight: Show us a new workflow, interaction, market or experiment.
Trust: Users should understand what the agent can do, what it may spend and what happened.
Product quality: The experience, demo and story should feel coherent and usable.
What will not stand out: a generic chat wrapper, a common RFH idea reproduced without new insight, a mocked payment presented as a transaction, or partner technology added only to qualify for a track.


2. Judging
What projects will be judged on
End-to-end functionality: Does the core flow work from intent to result?
Creativity and novelty: Is there an original idea, interaction or experiment?
User value and market feasibility: Does this solve something people would use or pay for?
Prava implementation: Is the transaction meaningful, reliable and central to the product?
Track implementation: Does the partner technology materially improve the experience?
Product experience: Is the product easy to understand, use and demonstrate?
What happens next: Could the project continue as a product, startup or valuable experiment?
Existing users, signups, transactions or interviews can strengthen a submission, but they are not required. Track winners must first meet the standard of a good hackathon project, then demonstrate a strong implementation of that track's technology.

3. Building with Prava
What is Prava?
Prava enables AI agents to complete transactions safely. It gives builders agent-native and application-native ways to create payment sessions, control how money may be used and complete commercial workflows.

Prava has official partnerships with card networks like Visa, Mastercard, Discover etc. Prava is backed by WTFund of Nikhil Kamath- India’s fintech billionaire & founder of largest stock broking platform- Zerodha.

Every official hackathon submission must use Prava meaningfully.

What counts as a meaningful implementation?
Prava enables a core action; it is not a payment button added at the end.
The agent discovers, decides, authorizes, pays, purchases or completes a commercial workflow.
The demo uses a working sandbox or test flow, not a mocked transaction.
The user can understand what the agent is doing and whether it succeeded.
If you claim an order was placed, show the completed checkout result. Creating a payment session alone is not a completed order.
Your team can explain why it chose MCP, CLI, hosted API or SDK/API.
Sandbox and production access
Anyone can sign up through the Prava Developer Dashboard and start building in the sandbox. Stay in the sandbox until your complete flow works reliably end to end.

5. Finding merchants
Use these resources to discover merchants with UCP, MCP or guest-checkout options:

Composio MCP Gateway - a directory of official MCP servers.
UCP Checker - many listed merchants may be compatible with Prava.
E-commerce MCP servers - an e-commerce-focused MCP directory.
Prava Merchant List - additional merchants, including some with MCP support.
Important: These are discovery resources, not guarantees of compatibility. Not every merchant has been tested end to end, and merchant support can change. Research your merchant and validate the complete flow early. Keep a backup merchant or flow if your first choice is unreliable.


6. Rules
Teams may have 1-4 accepted participants. Only registered team members may contribute.
Judged work must be completed during the official build window.
You may build on an existing product, but you must disclose what existed before the event and what was built during it.
Undisclosed pre-built work, plagiarism, hidden contributors, fake transactions, fabricated results or misleading demos may lead to disqualification.
AI development tools are allowed and encouraged. Your team must still understand and explain the submission.
Do not expose API keys, payment credentials or personal information in repositories or demos.
Follow Devfolio rules, the Code of Conduct, partner terms and applicable laws.
Judges may request a live demo, repository access, logs or transaction evidence.
Contact the Prava team before escalating an issue to a partner. We will route it to the right person.
7. Submission checklist
Before submitting, make sure you have:

A working demo and a short demo video.
A repository or judge-access link.
A clear explanation of the user, problem and product.
A clear explanation of the Prava integration and the transaction outcome.
The partner-track implementation and evidence, where applicable.
A disclosure of pre-existing work and what was built during the hackathon.
A short summary of what worked, what did not and what you learned.
Your submission should make it easy for a judge to understand the product, run or watch the core flow and verify the integrations.

How to submit on Devfolio
Open your hackathon dashboard and select Add Project.
Add your project name, tagline, problem, technologies used, links, demo video and screenshots. Your first screenshot becomes the cover image.
Clearly mention Prava and any track-specific technology in Technologies Used.
For teams, only the team admin can submit. Ensure every member has been added and completed the required RSVP/check-in.
You may save the project as a draft, but it is submitted only after you select Publish Project and its status shows Submitted.
